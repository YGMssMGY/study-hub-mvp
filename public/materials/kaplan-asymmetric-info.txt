Study Hub topic brief. Source: Kaplan course map (Ch 12). The Ch 12 slides are not in the Drive folder yet, so this is a general overview.

General overview (check it against Kaplan's lecture):
- Asymmetric information: one side of a trade knows more than the other.
- Adverse selection (hidden information before the deal): in a "lemons" used-car market, buyers can't tell good cars from bad, so they pay an average price and good cars leave the market. Insurance faces the same problem when high-risk buyers are most eager to buy.
- Moral hazard (hidden action after the deal): once insured, people may take less care.
- Fixes: signaling by the informed side (warranties, education credentials), screening by the uninformed side (deductibles, menus of contracts), reputation, mandates and disclosure rules.

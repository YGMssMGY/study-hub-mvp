Oligopoly: Bertrand and Cournot · ECON 351x, Prof. Matthew Hill (Exam 2)
Study Hub topic brief. Source: Hill Topics for Exam 2; Oligopoly slides; Oligopoly practice set.

- Bertrand (price competition, identical products): price falls to P = MC, the same as perfect competition.
- Cournot (quantity competition) with P = A − bQ and identical MC = c: each firm's MR_i = A − 2b·q_i − b·q_j. The symmetric solution is q = (A − c)/(3b) per firm and Q = 2(A − c)/(3b).
- Profit per firm = (P − MC) × q when there is no fixed cost.
- Ranking with the same demand and cost: Price: Monopoly > Cournot > Perfect competition = Bertrand. Quantity: Perfect competition > Cournot > Monopoly.
- Collusion (e.g. a price-fixing case) pushes oligopolists toward the monopoly outcome.

Practice example
P = 100 − Q, MC = 10. Cournot: q = (100 − 10)/3 = 30 each, Q = 60, P = 40, profit = (40 − 10) × 30 = 900 each.
Bertrand: P = 10, Q = 90.

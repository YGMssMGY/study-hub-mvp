Consumer choice and markets · ECON 351x, Prof. Matthew Hill (Exam 1)
Study Hub topic brief. Source: Hill Topics for Exam 1; Hill Intro to Econ slides.

Ch 4 Consumer Choice
- Preferences: what people like and how they rank options.
- Stated vs revealed preferences: what people say they want vs what their actual choices show. Economists trust revealed preferences.
- Mimetic desire and preference formation: people often want things because others want them.

Ch 5 Markets (overview)
- Allocation systems: traditional (custom decides), command and control (an authority decides), market (prices decide).
- Markets and innovation: prices reward producers who find cheaper or better ways to meet demand.

Hill's key ideas
- Economics studies how people respond to incentives under scarcity.
- Opportunity cost = the value of the next best alternative you give up (e.g. the time cost of a movie).
- Incentives create unintended consequences: a bounty on vipers led people to breed vipers; an NBA player stopped shooting to protect a 3-point bonus; a 1585 Antwerp food price ceiling led to shortages; rent control raised average rents.
- "Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome." "Policy outcomes don't care about policy intentions."

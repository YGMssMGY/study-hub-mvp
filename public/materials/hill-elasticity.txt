Elasticity · ECON 351x, Prof. Matthew Hill (Exam 1)
Study Hub topic brief. Source: Hill Topics for Exam 1; Hill Elasticity slides; Hill cheat sheet.

Own-price elasticity of demand
- E = %ΔQ ÷ %ΔP. Point form: E = (dQ/dP) × (P/Q).
- |E| > 1 elastic, |E| < 1 inelastic, |E| = 1 unit elastic. Compare the absolute value to 1; slope is not elasticity.
- Revenue test: if demand is elastic, a price cut raises total revenue; if inelastic, a price cut lowers it.
- On a linear demand curve, demand is elastic at high prices and inelastic at low prices.

Supply elasticity
- Same idea for the supply curve: %ΔQs ÷ %ΔP, interpreted as elastic, inelastic or unit elastic.

Both formulas are on the cheat sheet Hill allows in exams.

Practice example
Demand Q = 100 − 2P at P = 30: Q = 40, dQ/dP = −2, so E = −2 × 30/40 = −1.5. Demand is elastic, so lowering the price raises revenue.
At P = 20: Q = 60, E = −2 × 20/60 ≈ −0.67, inelastic.
